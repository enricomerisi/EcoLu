Setting the scene

Explaining the current state of plastic waste in Myanmar. Today the market is linear: most waste is produced by PMCs, consumed and discarded by PWPs, and ends up in landfill. Only in exceptional cases is PET picked by waste pickers and sold through a chain of middlemen before it reaches a recycling plant.

Plastic Manufacturing Companies (PMC)

Producers of plastic packaging and consumer goods.

Plastic Waste Producers (PWP)

Middle entities — restaurants, hospitals, households — that use PMCs' products, then consume and dispose of the plastic goods.

Waste Pickers

Informal workers who pick plastic waste to resell from waste sites across Myanmar.

Plastic Recycling Plants (PRP)

Entities demanding a reliable, quality, and standardised stream of raw rPET.

Service #1 · On-demand PWPs

EcoLu needs the cooperation of PWPs to collect clean, sorted PET waste.

Challenge

PWPs currently have no incentive, knowledge, or means to store, segregate, or sell their PET waste to us.

Incentive

PWPs are everywhere and produce a high volume of raw PET waste — an asset EcoLu will capture and monetise before it gets dumped.

Our service

We inform and equip PWPs to collect, store, and sell their PET waste to EcoLu, through on-demand pick-up and convincing incentives.

Service #2 · PWP community collection

Households, streets, and neighbourhoods make up a large slice of PWPs, and demand a tailored approach that differs from businesses.

Challenge

There is a lack of knowledge, recycling culture, and convincing incentives for households to recycle their plastic waste.

EcoLu's solution

We build accessible mobile PET collection systems where plastic can be traded for goods such as rice and cooking oil — incentivising household participation.

Value for waste pickers

More than just salaries — giving back to our workforce through socioeconomic mobility.

Challenge

The waste-picking sector is large — roughly 345 pickers per 100,000 citizens in Yangon, with a significant population of women and children. The current, competitive working model prevents them from organising as a cohesive workforce, and their livelihood is unreliable: income depends on how much plastic they can collect, and price depends on the middleman they can access.

EcoLu's solution

Empowering the waste pickers in our network through:

  • Training, equipment, and a reliable, fair salary
  • Official documents for lawful employment
  • Community, built through cooperative models
  • Reduced inequality through equal, fair employment

Who we serve

Three customer types complete EcoLu's circular model.

Customer

PRPs

International plastic recycling plants in Myanmar

Challenge

PRPs struggle to meet their PET waste input targets, and their current sources are unreliable, unprofessional, and informal.

EcoLu's service

We source recyclable PET in a reliable, structured system from otherwise-disregarded PWP sources — turning waste into value.

Customer

PMCs

A large international manufacturer trying to reach its ESG targets

Challenge

PMCs struggle to source cheap, clean, ready-to-use rPET; most only have access to expensive virgin PET imports, and struggle to meet ESG standards.

EcoLu's service

We source rPET from partner recycling plants for PMCs — achieving circularity and promoting community interdependence and economic growth.

Customer

Carbon & plastic credit buyers

International companies seeking high-integrity carbon and plastic credits

Challenge

International corporations face heavy pressure on sustainable compliance and ESGs, and offset their environmental impact by buying carbon and plastic credits.

EcoLu's service

We qualify for, and can provide, the highest and most sought-after tier of plastic credits — Tier 3 Circular Credits ($300–500/ton), verified closed-loop recycling back into equivalent products.

Next steps for EcoLu

Our needs and plans to proceed — flexible, and open to change as suited.

Main focus for the next 2 years

Implementing small-scale pilot programmes. Example goals: partner with one neighbourhood, or one manageable-sized stakeholder type (e.g. schools), and a small number of waste picker employees, to train and collect PET waste for a year — and establish a contract with a local PRP to sell the collected waste.

What it achieves

  • Lets us determine the costs and resources required
  • Establishes relationships in the community
  • Proves the feasibility of EcoLu's model
  • Identifies areas for adaptation and improvement
Year 1

Establishment

  1. Planning logistics to execute pilot programmes that prove the viability of our large-scale model
  2. Acquiring funding to enable pilot programmes' execution (humanitarian grants, sustainability competitions, investors)
  3. Building an online portfolio for EcoLu (website, social media presence)
  4. Collecting contacts for core operations — waste pickers, trash loaders, drivers, site managers, and vehicle arrangement
  5. Outreach to establish market interest and partnerships (community engagement, NGO outreach, UN, WFP)
  6. Acquiring the knowledge needed to become carbon- and plastic-credit certified
Year 2

Aiming to scale

  1. Implementing EcoLu's full operational model in Yangon
  2. Establishing our core operations workforce
  3. Acquiring more funding
  4. Establishing partnerships with large to medium PWPs — including NGOs and foreign government initiatives like the EU's — and developing the on-demand collection service
  5. Reaching carbon- and plastic-credit certification
  6. IT development: a platform/website for partners and the EcoLu team
  7. Marketing: brand positioning and storytelling
  8. Sales: expanding partnerships with PMCs and PRPs, and establishing potential clients
